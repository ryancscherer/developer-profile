# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Prospective clients — individuals or small businesses — sizing up whether Ryan has the range and polish to trust with a custom Android app or website build. Secondary: people who land on the page out of general curiosity about the apps themselves.

## Product Purpose

A personal landing page for Ryan (Scherer Developer), an independent Android and web developer based in Brisbane, Australia. It exists to prove range and craft through two real, shipped apps — Vicki the Manager and Pop Shot — rather than through claims, and to give a visitor a direct, low-friction way to get in touch about a project.

## Positioning

Ryan takes the complexity out of building apps and websites. The page's own structure argues this as a single continuous story, not a set of disconnected sections: it opens on two real, plain-language ideas (no tech words at all), shows those ideas resolving into simple features (shown horizontally, as pieces visibly coming together), then into the two complete real apps, then explains how the work actually happens (fast, transparent, accountable), and only reveals the technology stack at the very end, once trust is already built. The page is intentionally compact, this is a story told quickly, not a long scroll.

### The two real ideas (verbatim, used as the opening content)
- Vicki the Manager: "I want to manage the rotations of time on the soccer field, so that everyone gets an even amount of time. I want to focus on the game, not doing maths."
- Pop Shot: "I want a fun game that builds in complexity, but feels impossible to put down."

## Conversion & proof

- Primary and secondary CTA: primary is a direct mailto to Ryan; there is no secondary funnel step — the page is not built to capture leads through a form.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: this person turns a messy idea into something simple people actually use — and stands behind it.
- Belief ladder: (1) this is a real person with real shipped work, not a template → (2) he builds fast and stays accountable, so working with him won't feel like a black box → (3) the craft and UX quality on display is what my own project would get → (4) the tech underneath is genuinely current, confirmed only after trust is earned, not led with → (5) reaching out costs nothing; there's no sales-funnel pressure.
- Proof on hand: real screenshots from Vicki the Manager (a youth soccer coaching app), an illustrated build-up of Pop Shot (an arcade game), and both apps' real feature sets and tech stacks.
- **Trust/process statement** (client's own words, keep close to verbatim): builds quickly so the client can see exactly what's happening as it happens; stands behind what he builds, in every way he acts; builds trust first, so the client feels like they've found a partner, not a vendor; no confusing complexity — ideas turn into simple user features, then complete apps and mobile sites.

## Brand Personality

Direct, confident, unpretentious, craftsmanship-focused. First-person and plainspoken — "I'm Ryan," not a company voice. No corporate agency filler.

## Anti-references

Small studio/agency sites that lean on client-logo strips, stats/metrics bars, numbered "our process" sections, and "book a call now" urgency. Competitor research turned up this exact shape repeated everywhere in the small-studio space, dark skin or light — it's the pattern this page deliberately avoids, since a solo developer with no client roster has no honest way to back those signals up.

## Design Principles

Proof by demonstration, not by claim — real screenshots and real product detail stand in for adjectives. Showcase-first, not sales-funnel — no urgency, no persistent CTA, one calm point of contact. Range over volume — two very different, fully realized products say more than a long list of half-finished ones. Built in the subject's own material — the page borrows its typographic identity from code itself, since the subject is software. **Human language before technical language** — the page opens on the idea and the problem in plain words, resolves it into simple features, then into complete apps; the technology stack is real and current, but it is the last thing shown, not the first, because trust is earned by clarity and craft before it's earned by a buzzword list. **One story, told compactly** — every section reads as the next beat of the same narrative (idea, then feature, then app, then how it's built, then the tech), not a series of separate pitches; keep the page tight rather than padding it out with more sections.

### Copy constraint (client-specified, applies to all visible site text)
No hyphens and no em dashes anywhere in on-page copy, headings, captions, alt text, or button labels. Use periods, commas, or a restructured sentence instead. This does not apply to code, class names, file names, or standardized technical values (ISO codes, URLs).

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA contrast throughout. Respects `prefers-reduced-motion` — any scroll-driven or entrance animation must degrade to a static, fully-visible layout rather than leaving content hidden or mid-transition.
